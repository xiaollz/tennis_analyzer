## Core Claim

Tom works with a student, Chris, whose forehand looks externally functional but is blocked by a common amateur pattern:

```text
shoulder pulls a locked arm
  -> forearm does not supinate into the slot
  -> racket lag is placed or dragged instead of emerging
  -> the chain cannot pass energy to the next link
```

The correction is not a complicated technical rebuild. Tom uses a simple throwing / through-motion frame, close to "skimming a stone", to restore segment-by-segment release.

## Student Problem

| Observable | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Shoulder-led pull | The upper arm and shoulder drag the racket as a stiff unit. |
| Straight/locked arm during drop | The elbow loses a comfortable bend; the arm becomes one rigid lever. |
| Pat-the-dog fixation | The player tries to place a shape, which closes and locks the racket/arm too early. |
| Missing forearm supination | The racket does not enter the slot through arm rotation and inertia. |
| Placed lag / no natural lag | Lag is manufactured by position rather than produced by chain timing. |

This matches the broader TPA pulling-trap family: a player can have a reasonable-looking takeback and swing path, while the energy transfer is still blocked because one link is actively holding or dragging the next link.

---

## What "Through Motion" Means Here

In this video, through motion should not be read as "push the racket linearly through the ball."

Working definition:

```text
Through motion = a visible path result after a healthy throwing/release sequence.
```

The useful internal model is throwing or skimming a stone: the arm segments are allowed to rotate and release in order. The useful visual result is that the racket travels through the hitting zone with late acceleration instead of being dragged early by the shoulder, hand, or elbow.

## Before / After Markers

| Phase | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| Drop | Arm straightens and locks; racket is lowered mechanically. | Arm keeps a comfortable bend; drop looks elastic. |
| Slot | Little visible forearm supination; racket face is shaped by forced pat-the-dog. | Forearm supination appears as the racket enters slot; strings may become visible to the side camera. |
| Lag | Hand/shoulder drag the racket, so lag is a placed shape. | Racket head lags naturally after body/arm rotation starts. |
| Forward swing | Shoulder pulls a stiff unit. | Racket accelerates late through the chain. |
| Follow-through | Blocked or pushed look. | More continuous through path before the natural finish. |

## Relation To Push / Through / Pulling / Throwing

This video adds a concrete learner case to the existing project model:

| Term | In this video |
|---|---|
| **Throw / Release** | The correction model. Use the throwing pattern to let forearm/racket release instead of dragging. |
| **Through** | The downstream path result when the racket is released through the hitting zone. |
| **Push / Control** | Not the main topic. A small control fraction can exist, but dominant shoulder/arm control blocks release. |
| **Pulling Trap** | The main failure mode: shoulder-led pulling of a locked arm. |

Important distinction:

```text
healthy through = chain release creates a through path
fake through    = shoulder/hand/elbow drags the racket to manufacture a through path
```

## 2026-06-04 User Rewatch: Early Supination During Drop

The user rewatched this video after the 2026-06-02 racket-drop / separation finding and identified the most useful practical point:

```text
During racket lowering, start forearm supination early enough.
```

This is not a new cue to twist the wrist at contact. It clarifies timing:

```text
too late:
  racket stays standing / face path unresolved
  -> forward swing starts
  -> racket head drops suddenly
  -> forearm/wrist has to drag it up

better:
  unit turn / initial racket shape
  -> early separation + forearm supination while lowering
  -> racket enters slot with a comfortable bend
  -> forward swing can release through
```
